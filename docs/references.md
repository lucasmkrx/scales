# Musical and implementation references

These support the factual tuning and theory conventions. The explanatory copy
and implementation in Scales are original.

- Standard guitar tuning and interval relationships:
  https://www.fender.com/articles/setup/standard-tuning-how-eadgbe-came-to-be
- Common alternate guitar tunings:
  https://www.fender.com/articles/setup/alternate-guitar-tuning
- Seven-string guitar standard tuning (Ibanez maintenance manual, string 1 to 7):
  https://files.kraftmusic.com/media/ownersmanual/Ibanez_Guitar_Maintenance_Manual.pdf
- Seven-string drop A (SIT Strings):
  https://sitstrings.com/pages/help-center/what-is-drop-tuning-on-a-7-string-guitar
- Standard four-, five-, and six-string bass pitches:
  https://www.studybass.com/gear/bass-tuning/bass-guitar-tunings/
- Diatonic chords and Roman-numeral analysis:
  https://www.studybass.com/lessons/harmony/what-are-major-scale-diatonic-chords/
- Chord tones in bass lines:
  https://www.studybass.com/lessons/harmony/chord-tones-in-basslines/
- E harmonic minor and the raised seventh:
  https://www.fender.com/articles/scales/harmonic-minor-guitar-scale
- Scale structures and mode terminology:
  https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/diatonic-modes/
- Scale-collection reference and names (names vary by source and tradition):
  https://ianring.com/musictheory/scales/
- Guitar/bass standard pitch uses scientific pitch notation, with middle C = C4.
  The PDF guides show sounding pitches rather than the octave transposition
  commonly used in notation for these instruments.

The tuning list is curated, not a popularity ranking. Lowered and drop variants
are specified by their actual open pitches. The melodic minor catalog uses the
ascending/jazz form in both directions; it does not imply classical descending
natural minor. Modal and non-diatonic progressions can require choices beyond
one key-scale label; the guides emphasize listening and chord tones.

  https://theory.stanford.edu/~blynn/sound/karplusstrong.html

instrument samples or additional sample licenses are used.

## Regional languages and musical terminology

The interface and generated scale atlases use American English (`en-US`),
Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`), and Latin American Spanish (`es-419`). Existing
short URL values `en`, `pt`, and `es` identify those editions. Regional language
values normalize to the same editions in shared links and saved preferences.

The terminology review covers the interface, all 67 named scales and arpeggios,
all collection families, tuning labels, and generated PDF instructions. It
preserves each collection's intervals and stable identifier. The four supplied
lesson books are English and Portuguese editions. Spanish users can generate
scale-reference PDFs for any supported setup.

- [W3C language-tag guidance](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/language-tags/)
  identifies `es-419` as Spanish used in Latin America.
- [Berklee PULSE scales and arpeggios](https://pulse.berklee.edu/content/practice/pdfs/PR_All_Scales_Arp_Treble%20Clef.pdf)
  supports the American English scale, mode, and arpeggio terminology.
- [José Rodríguez Alvira's seventh-chord reference](https://www.teoria.com/es/referencia/s/septima-acordes.php)
  and [minor-scale lesson](https://www.teoria.com/es/aprendizaje/escalas/04-menores.php)
  support Spanish chord qualities and the distinction between natural, harmonic,
  and melodic minor.
- [Universidad Autónoma de Chihuahua solfège guide](https://uach.mx/assets/media/publications/2023/4/5380_guias-de-estudio/fa_guia_de_solfeo.pdf)
  supports Latin American note names, accidentals, and semitone terminology.
- [INBAL harmony study guide](https://sgeia.inba.gob.mx/archivos/escuelas/musica/2024/esm_guia-armonia-sup-1_2024.pdf)
  supports the distinction between a tonal tonic and a chord's fundamental.
- [Sumarsam's introduction to Javanese gamelan](https://sumarsam.faculty.wesleyan.edu/files/2023/01/1_Introduction_to_Javanese_Gamelan.pdf)
  explains pelog tuning and five-note subsets. The app explicitly labels its
  pentatonic collection as an approximation in twelve-tone equal temperament.

Portuguese and Spanish distinguish the scale tonic (`tônica`, `tónica`) from the
chord root (`fundamental`). The interface uses that distinction when selecting
arpeggios, and the diagrams and generated atlases use the matching marker. The
melodic minor entry explicitly uses the ascending/jazz form in both directions.
Diminished seventh retains `1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭♭7`, including the interval spelling of its
seventh. A–G note letters and scientific pitch notation remain consistent across
languages, with a solfège key in Portuguese and Spanish help. Enharmonic display
names describe equal-tempered sounding pitches. The degree formulas retain the
interval relationships, including double flats.
